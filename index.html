URBAN EXPANSION AND HOUSING QUALITY IN AKURE METROPOLIS, ONDO STATE, NIGERIA Abstract Rapid urban growth in Nigerian cities has long been tied to declining residential quality, particularly in cities experiencing unplanned expansion. This paper examines the relationship between urban expansion and housing quality in Akure metropolis, focusing on the physical condition of dwellings, access to basic infrastructure, and the spatial patterns of residential deterioration in emerging peripheral settlements. 1.0 Introduction Akure, the capital of Ondo State, has experienced significant territorial growth over the past three decades, driven by rural-urban migration, natural population increase, and the expansion of commercial and administrative activities. What was once a compact, manageable urban settlement has grown into a sprawling metropolis, with development pushing outward into formerly agricultural land at the city's fringe. This pattern of outward growth is not unique to Akure. Across sub-Saharan Africa, cities are expanding faster than the capacity of urban authorities to deliver housing, roads, water, and sanitation to new residents (UN-Habitat, 2020). In Nigeria specifically, rapid urbanisation has consistently outpaced infrastructure provision, resulting in a widening gap between housing demand and the availability of decent, affordable shelter (Jiboye, 2011). References Jiboye, A. D. (2011). Sustainable urbanisation: Issues and challenges for effective urban governance in Nigeria. Journal of Sustainable Development, 4(6), 211–220. UN-Habitat. (2020). World cities report 2020: The value of sustainable urbanisation. United Nations Human Settlements Programme.
